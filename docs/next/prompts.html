Build your own

Clear beats long.

More context isn't better context. Unrelated details pull slides off topic, and conflicting asks force guesses. Give the skill the few things only you know; it handles research, structure and design.

Say the job

Who it's for and what they should decide or learn. That one line shapes the structure more than anything else.

Give the source of truth

Attach the files that matter. One right document beats ten loosely related ones, and it outranks the web.

Name the must-haves

Length, sections you need, things to avoid. Skip adjectives like "amazing"; they don't change the result.

Long but unclear

Make a really good, detailed, professional, modern deck about EVs. Include everything: the history of cars, batteries, Tesla, China, policies, charging, startups. Make it look premium and colourful with lots of charts and keep it short but comprehensive.

  • No audience and no decision to support
  • "Everything" means no focus
  • Short and comprehensive contradict

Short and clear

Make a 30-slide analysis of India's electric two-wheeler market for our leadership team, who must decide whether to enter in 2027. Use the attached market report first. One slide per major brand, and end with a go or no-go recommendation.

  • Audience and decision set the story
  • The source of truth is named
  • Must-haves are specific; design is left to the skill
