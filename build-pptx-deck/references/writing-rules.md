# Writing rules for slide copy

Slides are read in seconds by people who are also listening. Write like a careful person explaining something they understand to someone they respect.

## Headlines

- A headline is a full sentence that states the slide's point. "Churn peaks in month two on every plan", not "Churn analysis".
- Sentence case. No full stop needed. One or two lines; three is too long (about 110 characters is the ceiling).
- Put the number in the headline when the number is the point.
- Read all headlines in order. If they do not tell the story on their own, fix the storyline before the slides.
- Cover, section, agenda, recap, sources and closing slides may use short label titles.

## Body copy

- One idea per slide. If a slide needs "and also", it is two slides.
- Prefer a specific fact to an adjective. "Delivery in 2 days" beats "fast delivery".
- Every number carries its unit, its period and, on data slides, its source. "£3.9m revenue, Q3 2026".
- Keep parallel items parallel: same grammatical form, similar length.
- Card and bullet text is a phrase or one short sentence. Paragraphs belong in speaker notes.
- Define a term the first time it appears, or leave it out.
- Use the audience's words. An executive deck says "revenue"; a learning deck may need "the money coming in".

## Banned patterns (lint checks these)

- Em dashes, and en dashes or double hyphens used as punctuation. Use a comma, colon, full stop or brackets. An en dash in a number range such as 18–28% is fine.
- Exclamation marks and emoji.
- "Not just X, but Y", "It's not X, it's Y", "more than just", and other set-up-then-reveal constructions. Say Y.
- Self-answered questions: "The result? Faster delivery."
- Stock words: delve, leverage, seamless, robust, cutting-edge, game-changer, unlock, unleash, elevate, empower, harness, tapestry, testament, pivotal, crucial, paradigm, synergy, holistic, revolutionize, transformative, supercharge, world-class, best-in-class, next-level, ever-evolving, fast-paced, landscape, realm, foster, streamline, deep dive.
- Filler openers and closers: "In today's world", "It's worth noting", "At the end of the day", "In conclusion", "Moreover", "Furthermore".
- Groups of three used for rhythm when there are really two things or four.
- Hedging stacked on hedging ("could potentially help to"). Hedge once, where the uncertainty is.
- Title Case Headlines.

## Speaker notes

- Write what the presenter would say, in plain spoken sentences. Two to five sentences per slide.
- Add what the slide does not show: the reasoning, the caveat, the example.
- End with a short bridge to the next slide when it helps ("Next: where the money goes.").
- Put method details and assumptions in notes or the appendix, not on the slide.

## Density limits

| Element | Limit per slide |
|---|---|
| Bullets | 6, each under about 18 words |
| Cards | 8 (3 or 4 read best) |
| Table rows | 10, with 6 columns or fewer |
| Chart series | 4 for lines, 5 for stacked bars |
| Timeline milestones | 7 |
| Process or runbook steps | 6 |

Text that shrinks below about 12 pt on a card or row means the slide holds too much. Split it, or move detail to the notes.

When content exceeds a limit, split it across slides with a shared headline stem, or move detail to the appendix.

## Honesty

- Use only facts you found or were given. Mark gaps with a bracketed placeholder and report them.
- Quotes are real words from a named, real source, or they are not on the slide.
- If sources disagree, say so on the slide or in the notes and name both.
- Date anything that will age: "as of October 2026".
